Lesson 11 — Claims, Tone & Legal Guardrails

This section is about one thing:

Trustworthy language.

The report recommends that AA WIGS adopt a very specific editorial tone:

Calm rather than alarmist.
Precise rather than dramatic.
Buyer-centered rather than competitor-centered.
Evidence-led rather than self-congratulatory.
Transparent about natural variation and limitations.
Luxurious without using "luxury" as a substitute for proof.
Helpful before promotional.
Direct about policy conditions without sounding punitive.

Read those again.

That list is almost a brand personality guide.

What Should the Reader Feel?

The report says that after reading an AA WIGS article, the customer should think:

"I understand how to evaluate the product and what AA WIGS does."

The customer should not leave thinking:

"AA WIGS is saying every other company is dishonest."

That distinction is incredibly important.

You're building trust by explaining your own standards—not by attacking others.

Why This Matters

Imagine two luxury jewelers.

Jeweler A

"Everyone else sells fake diamonds."

Jeweler B

"Here's how we inspect every diamond before it reaches a customer."

Which one feels more professional?

Exactly.

The report wants AA WIGS to sound like Jeweler B.

The FTC Principle

The report reminds you of a core advertising principle:

Claims should be:

truthful,
not deceptive,
supported by evidence.

That applies not only to advertisements, but also to educational content that promotes AA WIGS products.
