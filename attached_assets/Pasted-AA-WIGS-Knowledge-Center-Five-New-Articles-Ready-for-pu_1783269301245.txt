What '180% Density' Actually Means — And Why It Changes Everything
Category: Wig Education  |  Reading Time: 4 min
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Model wearing the 22" Swiss HD Body Wave — crown fullness side profile ]
⸻
When you shop for a luxury wig, you'll see density percentages everywhere. 130%. 150%. 180%. 250%. The numbers keep climbing, and most buyers assume higher is always better — but that assumption leads to wigs that look unnatural, feel heavy, and don't hold their style.
At AA WIGS, every signature piece is crafted at 180% density. Not by default. By deliberate choice.
This article explains exactly what density means, why it matters more than most buyers realize, and why 180% is the specific standard that defines the AA WIGS signature piece.
What Density Actually Measures
Wig density refers to the amount of hair wefts sewn into the wig cap relative to a full human head of hair. That baseline — 100% — represents the average thickness of a natural head of hair.
So when a wig is labeled 180% density, it means the wig carries 80% more hair than a natural head. The result is a fuller, more voluminous look — but one that still moves, falls, and behaves the way real hair does.
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Close-up detail shot of wig crown showing density and natural movement ]
Why Lower Density Fails
130% and 150% density wigs are common in the market because they're cheaper to produce. Less hair, lower cost. But the trade-off is visible:
—  The crown looks thin, especially under natural or studio light
—  The lace perimeter can appear see-through where the hair doesn't cover it
—  Volume flattens within hours of styling
—  The wig loses its shape quickly with regular wear
These aren't quality failures in the traditional sense — they're predictable outcomes of insufficient density. A 130% wig isn't a bad product. It's simply the wrong product for a client who expects the look and feel of a full, natural head of hair.
Why Higher Density Isn't Always Better
On the other end, 250% and above density wigs carry so much hair that they stop behaving naturally. The weight becomes noticeable. The movement becomes stiff. The lace lies flat at the perimeter instead of blending into the skin. And the wig often has to be thinned by a stylist just to look right — which defeats the purpose of paying for premium density in the first place.
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Two-image comparison: natural side profile showing body wave movement ]
Why AA WIGS Chose 180%
180% density sits in the precise range where fullness and realism coexist. At this density:
—  The crown appears naturally full without looking heavy or staged
—  The body wave pattern holds its shape and bounce throughout the day
—  The lace perimeter blends cleanly because the hairline is dense enough to appear natural
—  The wig moves with the client — not against them
This is not a marketing number. It's the result of a deliberate production standard applied consistently to every AA WIGS signature piece.
AA WIGS TIP:  When examining any wig's density, hold it up to natural light and look at the crown. Thin coverage at the top is the clearest sign of under-density — no matter what the label says.
What This Means For You
If you've ever worn a wig that looked flat by midday, lost volume after the first wash, or never quite looked full enough — density was likely the reason.
The 22" AA WIGS Signature Swiss HD Body Wave is built at 180% density because that is the standard that produces the result our clients expect: a piece that looks full, moves naturally, and holds its quality over time.

How To Install A Glueless Wig For A Natural Hairline
Category: Beginner Guide  |  Reading Time: 5 min
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Model with natural hairline — front-facing, lace blended, hair styled ]
⸻
The number one reason women hesitate to buy their first luxury wig isn't price. It's fear.
Fear that it won't look natural. Fear that they won't know how to put it on. Fear that they'll spend hundreds of dollars on something that looks obviously like a wig.
That fear is understandable — and it's preventable. With the right technique, a glueless HD lace wig can be installed at home, look completely natural, and stay secure without adhesive.
This guide walks you through the complete process, step by step.
What 'Glueless' Actually Means
A glueless wig is designed to be worn without glue, tape, or bonding adhesive. Instead of chemical attachment, it uses:
—  An adjustable drawstring band at the nape for a secure, custom fit
—  Built-in combs at the front, sides, and nape for additional grip
—  Elastic ear tabs that grip naturally along the temples
The AA WIGS Signature Piece is glueless-ready out of the box. No additional tools, adhesives, or salon appointments required.
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Detail shot of wig cap interior showing adjustable band and comb placement ]
What You'll Need
—  Your AA WIGS signature piece
—  Edge control or light-hold gel (for hairline blending, optional)
—  A wide-tooth comb or paddle brush
—  Bobby pins
—  A headband or scarf to lay the edges
Step 1 — Prepare Your Natural Hair
Flat braiding your natural hair before installation gives you the smoothest base and keeps the wig lying flat throughout the day.
Braid your hair in a flat, cornrow pattern working toward the back of your head. For very short or thin hair, a stocking cap can be used instead. The goal is a smooth, flat foundation with no bumps that would show through the wig.
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Flat braid preparation — side view, natural hair prepped ]
Step 2 — Position The Wig
Hold the wig at the front two corners of the lace. Tilt your head slightly forward. Place the front of the wig at your natural hairline — not above it, not below it. At it.
Slide the wig back until the lace sits flush against your forehead. Your natural hairline should just peek through or sit directly beneath the lace.
Step 3 — Secure The Fit
With the wig positioned correctly, reach to the back and tighten the adjustable drawstring band until the wig sits firmly on your head without pulling or tension. It should feel secure, not tight.
Secure the front combs by pressing them gently into the hair at your temples. Clip the nape comb into the hair at the back. If needed, use one or two bobby pins along the perimeter for extra hold.
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Securing the wig — back three-quarter view showing band and ear tabs ]
Step 4 — Cut and Lay The Lace
Using small, sharp scissors, trim the excess lace as close to the hairline as possible. Cut in small sections, following the natural curve of your hairline. Take your time — this step makes the difference between a natural and obvious install.
Once trimmed, apply a small amount of edge control along the perimeter and use a soft brush to press the lace flat. Wrap your hairline with a silk scarf for 5–10 minutes to allow the lace to mold to your skin.
AA WIGS TIP:  Cut lace in good lighting. Natural daylight is best. Small, precise cuts are always better than one long cut that's hard to correct.
Step 5 — Style As Desired
Remove the scarf. The lace should now sit flush with no visible edge. Part the hair using the end of a comb, apply any desired product, and style the body wave to your preference.
The 13×6 lace frontal gives you a full six inches of parting space from ear to ear — enough for a deep side part, a center part, or a pulled-back style.
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Final styled result — front facing, natural hairline, body wave styled ]
Common Mistakes To Avoid
—  Placing the wig too far back — creates an unnatural gap at the forehead
—  Cutting too much lace at once — work in small sections
—  Skipping the scarf step — the lace won't lay flat without it
—  Over-applying edge gel — less is more when blending HD lace

How To Choose The Right Wig Color For Your Skin Tone
Category: Style Guide  |  Reading Time: 4 min
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Model wearing Natural Black body wave — natural lighting, full front view ]
⸻
Color is personal. What looks stunning on one woman can feel flat or off on another — not because the wig is wrong, but because the shade doesn't align with her skin's undertones.
Understanding how color interacts with your complexion takes the guesswork out of choosing. This guide walks you through how to read your undertone and apply it to your wig selection with confidence.
Start With Your Undertone, Not Your Skin Tone
Skin tone — fair, medium, deep — describes the surface of your skin. Undertone describes the warmth beneath it. And undertone is the variable that actually determines which hair colors look natural and which ones don't.
There are three undertones:
—  Warm — golden, peachy, or yellow beneath the surface
—  Cool — pink, red, or bluish beneath the surface
—  Neutral — a balance of warm and cool, no single tone dominates
How To Identify Your Undertone
Three simple methods:
The Vein Test — Look at the veins on the inside of your wrist in natural light. Green or olive veins indicate a warm undertone. Blue or purple veins indicate a cool undertone. A mix of both suggests neutral.
The Jewelry Test — Gold jewelry tends to complement warm undertones. Silver tends to complement cool undertones. If both look equally good on you, you're likely neutral.
The White vs. Cream Test — Hold a bright white fabric next to your face, then an off-white or cream. If bright white makes your face look fresher, you're cool. If cream looks more flattering, you're warm.
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Flat lay detail shot of Natural Black wig on ivory surface — showing depth and sheen ]
Natural Black And Your Undertone
Natural Black — the shade of the AA WIGS Signature Piece — is one of the most universally wearable shades in hair because of its depth and neutrality. That said, it still interacts differently depending on your undertone.
Warm undertones: Natural Black provides a rich contrast that enhances golden and olive complexions. The depth of the color makes warm skin appear luminous. This combination photographs particularly well in natural and golden light.
Cool undertones: Natural Black aligns naturally with cooler complexions, especially deeper skin tones with blue or red undertones. The color has enough depth to feel intentional rather than harsh against cool-toned skin.
Neutral undertones: Natural Black is one of the easiest shades for neutral undertones to wear. The color doesn't pull warm or cool, so it adapts to the skin rather than competing with it.
AA WIGS TIP:  Natural lighting is the truest test of any color against your skin. If you're unsure, take a photo outside in daylight before making a final decision.
What About The Hairline?
With a 13×6 Swiss HD Lace frontal, the lace blends into virtually every skin tone because of how fine and transparent the lace construction is. Unlike thicker lace that can appear as a visible strip, Swiss HD Lace mimics the look of hair growing directly from the scalp.
This means the color of the wig doesn't have to compensate for a visible lace line — the lace disappears on its own, leaving only the hair color to complement your skin.
When You're Between Two Shades
If you're considering AA WIGS but unsure whether Natural Black is the right fit for you, ask yourself one question: does Natural Black hair look natural on women with a similar complexion to mine?
Natural Black is worn across every skin tone and undertone because it's the closest shade to natural, unprocessed human hair. It doesn't require styling to look intentional — it looks deliberate from the moment it's installed.

The Cuticle Is Everything: What Virgin Human Hair Actually Preserves
Category: Quality Education  |  Reading Time: 5 min
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Close-up flat lay of body wave texture on ivory surface — natural sheen, wave pattern visible ]
⸻
Most conversations about wig quality start and end with labels. Virgin. Remy. 100% Human Hair. But labels don't tell you what's happening at the microscopic level — and that's exactly where the quality of a wig is determined.
The single most important structure in any strand of human hair is the cuticle. Understanding what it does, what damages it, and what virgin hair does to preserve it is the clearest path to understanding why two wigs with the same label can perform completely differently.
What The Cuticle Actually Is
The cuticle is the outermost layer of every strand of human hair. Under a microscope, it looks like overlapping scales — similar to roof shingles layered from root to tip. Each scale lies flat and points downward toward the end of the strand.
This structure serves three functions:
—  It seals moisture inside the hair shaft, keeping the strand soft and pliable
—  It reflects light evenly, which is what creates the natural shine of healthy hair
—  It protects the inner cortex — the part of the hair that determines strength, elasticity, and color
When the cuticle is intact, hair behaves the way you expect it to: it moves naturally, holds its texture, responds to conditioning, and maintains its appearance over time.
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Macro detail of body wave strand — showing natural light reflection and texture ]
What Damages The Cuticle
Chemical processes are the primary cause of cuticle damage. Every time hair is bleached, colored, relaxed, or permed, the process requires lifting or breaking down the cuticle layer to alter the hair's structure or pigment.
A single chemical process causes measurable cuticle damage. Multiple processes compound that damage. Hair that has been bleached and then dyed, or relaxed and then colored, has a cuticle that is partially or entirely compromised before it ever reaches the consumer.
The consequence isn't visible on the shelf — manufacturers often coat processed hair with silicone or other synthetic polymers to simulate the smooth, shiny appearance of a healthy cuticle. That coating washes off within the first few shampoos, and the underlying damage becomes apparent:
—  Texture changes — the hair stiffens, loses its softness, or becomes coarse
—  Tangling increases — damaged cuticle scales catch on one another instead of lying flat
—  Shine disappears — the silicone coating is gone, and the damaged cuticle scatters light unevenly
—  Moisture retention drops — the compromised cuticle can no longer seal hydration inside the shaft
AA WIGS TIP:  The first wash is the most reliable test of cuticle integrity. Virgin hair maintains its texture and feel after washing. Hair that changes significantly after the first shampoo — stiffening, losing shine, or tangling unexpectedly — was likely processed before it reached you.
What Virgin Hair Preserves
Virgin human hair is hair that has never been chemically processed. The cuticle has not been lifted, stripped, or coated. Every scale is intact, lying flat in its natural direction from root to tip.
This means:
—  Moisture retention remains at its natural level — the hair stays soft without requiring heavy product
—  Light reflection is consistent — the natural shine is structural, not cosmetic
—  Heat and color response is predictable — the hair behaves the way natural unprocessed hair does
—  Longevity is substantially longer — there is no pre-existing chemical stress shortening the hair's lifespan
Virgin hair doesn't perform better because of a label. It performs better because the structure that determines performance has never been compromised.
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Side profile model shot — body wave movement showing natural light interaction ]
Virgin vs. Remy: Understanding The Distinction
These two terms describe different things and are often confused.
Remy refers to how the hair was collected and sorted. In Remy hair, every strand runs in the same direction — root to tip — which prevents the cuticle scales on adjacent strands from catching against each other and causing matting. Remy is a collection standard.
Virgin refers to the chemical history of the hair. Virgin hair has never been processed, regardless of how it was collected.
Hair can be Remy without being virgin — cuticle-aligned but chemically treated. Hair can be virgin without being perfectly Remy, though reputable producers collect and sort virgin hair in a Remy manner as standard practice.
The highest standard combines both: virgin Remy hair — chemically unprocessed and cuticle-aligned from root to tip. This is the standard applied to every AA WIGS signature piece.
How To Verify Cuticle Integrity Before You Commit
When evaluating any wig — including a sample unit — these are the physical tests that reveal cuticle condition:
The slide test: Run two strands between your fingers from root to tip, then tip to root. Healthy cuticles feel smooth in both directions. Damaged cuticles feel noticeably rougher when moving against the grain.
The shed test: Gently run your fingers through a small section. Some shedding is normal. Excessive shedding from minimal handling indicates cuticle damage at the root end where strands break most easily.
The wash test: Wash a small section with sulfate-free shampoo and allow it to air dry without product. Virgin hair returns to its natural texture. Processed hair changes — often becoming drier, coarser, or losing its pattern.
The stretch test: Take a single wet strand and gently stretch it. Healthy hair with an intact cuticle has natural elasticity and returns to its original length. Hair with a compromised cortex (often from chemical processing) breaks rather than stretches.
Why The Cuticle Is Non-Negotiable At AA WIGS
Every specification of the AA WIGS signature piece — the 13×6 Swiss HD Lace, the 180% density, the body wave texture — depends on the cuticle being intact to perform as intended.
The body wave pattern holds because the hair fiber is strong enough to maintain a heat-set texture over time. The natural shine reads as authentic because light is reflecting off an undamaged surface. The longevity is real because there is no pre-existing damage accelerating the hair's deterioration.
Sourcing virgin human hair isn't a premium add-on. It's the foundation that makes every other quality standard possible.

How To Refresh Your Body Wave Between Washes Without Ruining The Curl Pattern
Category: Wig Care  |  Reading Time: 4 min
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Model with refreshed body wave — defined wave pattern, healthy sheen ]
⸻
Washing your wig every few days isn't realistic — and it isn't necessary. Over-washing virgin human hair actually accelerates wear, strips the hair's natural moisture, and causes the body wave pattern to loosen faster than it should.
The key to maintaining your AA WIGS signature piece between washes isn't more product. It's the right technique, applied consistently, to revive the wave without stressing the hair.
Here's exactly how to do it.
Why Body Wave Loses Its Pattern Between Washes
Body wave is a heat-set texture applied to the hair during production. Over time — through wear, sleeping, and environmental factors — the wave pattern compresses and stretches. The hair doesn't lose its texture permanently. It just needs to be reminded of it.
The two main factors that flatten body wave between washes are:
—  Friction — from sleeping, styling, or rubbing against clothing
—  Dryness — dry hair can't hold a wave because the fiber contracts and loses elasticity
Both are addressable without a full wash cycle.
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Product flat lay on ivory surface — leave-in conditioner and water spray ]
The Between-Wash Refresh Routine
Step 1 — Mist, Don't Soak
Fill a small spray bottle with water or a diluted leave-in conditioner mix (roughly 80% water, 20% leave-in). Lightly mist the hair section by section until it's damp — not wet. The goal is to reintroduce just enough moisture to reactivate the wave.
Step 2 — Detangle From Ends To Roots
Before touching the wave pattern, remove any tangles using a wide-tooth comb. Always start from the ends and work slowly upward toward the roots. Attempting to detangle from the root down causes unnecessary breakage and disrupts the wave pattern from the base.
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Close-up of finger combing technique — showing wave definition returning ]
Step 3 — Finger Comb The Wave Back
Once detangled, put the comb down. Using clean fingers, gently scrunch the hair upward section by section, encouraging the wave to reform. Work from underneath the hair, pressing upward rather than downward. This technique lifts the wave from the base rather than flattening it from the top.
Step 4 — Apply Leave-In Lightly
If the hair still feels dry after misting, apply a small amount of leave-in conditioner to your palms and smooth it through the mid-lengths and ends only. Avoid the roots and lace area — product buildup at the root line shortens the time between necessary washes.
AA WIGS TIP:  Less is always more when refreshing. Over-applying product between washes creates buildup that dulls the hair and weighs down the wave. Start with the minimum and add only if needed.
Step 5 — Air Dry Or Diffuse
Allow the hair to air dry fully before styling or wearing. If you're short on time, use a diffuser attachment on the lowest heat setting, scrunching the hair upward as you dry. Avoid brushing or combing while wet — this stretches the wave and prevents it from reforming correctly.
How Often Should You Refresh?
Most clients find that a light refresh every 2–3 days maintains the body wave pattern comfortably between full wash cycles.
A full wash should happen every 7–14 days depending on how frequently the wig is worn, how much product is used, and environmental conditions like heat and humidity.
What To Avoid
—  Brushing dry body wave — always detangle when damp to preserve the pattern
—  Using heavy oils or butters as a refresh product — they coat the hair rather than moisturize it
—  Applying heat directly to damp hair — wait until fully dry or use a heat protectant
—  Sleeping in the wig without protection — always wrap in a silk or satin scarf before sleep
Protecting The Wave Overnight
The most effective thing you can do to extend your refresh routine is protect the wig at night. Wrap the hair loosely in a silk or satin scarf, or store the wig on a satin-lined surface inside its box. This prevents the friction that compresses the wave while you sleep.
A client who protects her wig overnight will consistently need fewer refreshes and less product — and the wig will hold its texture significantly longer.
