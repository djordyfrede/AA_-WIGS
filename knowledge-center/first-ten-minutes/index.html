Why Inspect Before Installing?

Once a wig's lace is cut, the cap is washed, or chemical services are applied, determining the original condition of the unit becomes significantly more difficult. Many common concerns — shedding, color variation, lace condition — look different before and after installation.

This inspection takes ten minutes. It gives you a clear, documented record of your unit at arrival. If everything is correct, you proceed with confidence. If something concerns you, you have photographic evidence and an unaltered unit — the strongest position from which to contact support.

Important: Do not cut the lace, apply glue or tape, wash, color, bleach knots, pluck, or heat style until this inspection is complete and you are satisfied with the unit. These actions are permanent and may affect your ability to request support for pre-existing issues.

Step 1 — Prepare Your Inspection Space

1

Choose good light

Natural daylight near a window is ideal. If not available, use consistent white artificial light. Avoid warm-toned bulbs — they make dark colors appear warmer and can mask color discrepancies.

2

Have a wig stand or mannequin head ready

Inspecting the wig flat on a table limits your ability to assess density and drape. A stand lets you evaluate how the hair falls and how the lace lies.

3

Prepare your phone for photos

You'll photograph each stage of the inspection. Clear space in your camera roll before you begin.

Step 2 — Photograph the Package Before Opening

This step takes thirty seconds and creates a timestamped record of the package's condition at delivery — before you have touched the product.

1

Photograph the outer shipping box

Show all six sides if possible. Note any visible damage — dents, tears, wet spots — in the photos before opening.

2

Photograph the sealed inner packaging

Before opening the burgundy box or removing any wrapping, photograph it intact. This establishes the product arrived sealed.

Why this matters: If a delivery issue occurred during transit, these photographs document it happened before you opened the package — not after.

Step 3 — Open and Confirm Contents

Remove each item from the packaging carefully and lay everything out before assessing the wig itself. Confirm that all included items are present. For a full list of what should be in your AA WIGS package, see What's in the AA WIGS Box.

Photograph the full unboxed contents before handling individual items.

Step 4 — Inspect the Lace

The lace is the most delicate part of the wig and the area customers most frequently question on arrival.

1

Hold the lace up to natural light

Swiss HD lace is designed to be very fine and sheer. When held to light, you should be able to see through it. This is normal — it is not a defect.

2

Check for tears or holes

Run your finger gently along the lace area from ear to ear. Tears or holes in the lace that were present at delivery — before cutting — should be photographed and reported before any alteration.

3

Check the lace edge

The lace should have a clean, consistent edge. Slight waviness is normal. Note any fraying or uneven edges in your photos.

Lace — Quick Reference
🟢 Normal
Sheer appearance when held to light · Slight waviness at edge · Faint knot visibility before tinting
🟡 Monitor
Minor fraying at outer edge · Faint discoloration that may be from packaging
🔴 Contact Support
Tears or holes in the lace area · Lace does not match described type · Significant discoloration

Step 5 — Inspect the Cap Construction

1

Check the weft stitching

Turn the cap inside out and examine where the wefts are sewn onto the cap. Stitching should be even and secure. Loose or missing stitches at arrival — before any wear — should be photographed.

2

Check adjustable straps and combs

Confirm that all adjustable straps move freely and that any combs or clips are securely attached to the cap.

3

Check the elastic band

The elastic band around the perimeter should be intact and consistent in tension. No breaks or gaps.

Step 6 — Confirm Color Accuracy

Color in photographs can appear differently depending on screen brightness, lighting, and post-processing. Evaluate color under natural light against your order confirmation, not against your phone or computer screen.

1

Compare under natural light

Take the wig to a window. Natural daylight is the most accurate reference. Compare the color of the weft to the color shown in your confirmation photos under the same daylight.

2

Check for consistency throughout

The color should be consistent from root to tip and consistent across all wefts. Natural human hair has tonal variation — this is expected. A significant shift from a cool-toned color to a warm-toned result is worth photographing.

Color — Quick Reference
🟢 Normal
Slight tonal variation root to tip · Appears slightly different from product photo on different screens · Natural hair variation across wefts
🟡 Monitor
Slightly cooler or warmer than expected — photograph in daylight before deciding
🔴 Contact Support
Clearly wrong color family · Significant inconsistency between wefts · Color does not match order specification

Step 7 — Measure the Length

Wig length is measured from the crown of the cap to the ends of the straightened hair — not in its natural wave pattern. Body wave wigs will appear shorter than their stated length when the wave is intact, because the wave compresses the hair.

1

Place the wig on a stand

Measure from the top center of the cap down to the ends of the longest hair using a soft tape measure. Gently straighten a section of hair to measure — do not pull.

2

Allow ±0.5–1 inch variance

Human hair length is measured from one natural growing point to another and trimmed to a consistent length. A small variance of up to one inch from the stated length is within manufacturing standard.

Length — Quick Reference
🟢 Normal
Appears shorter than stated length when wave is intact · Within ±1 inch when measured straightened
🔴 Contact Support
More than 1.5 inches shorter than stated length when hair is straightened for measurement

Step 8 — Check Density and Shedding

1

Assess density visually

Place the wig on a stand and assess the overall fullness. At 180% density, the wig should appear full from root to mid-length. Hold a white sheet of paper behind sections of hair — sparse areas will be clearly visible against white.

2

Check shedding with a gentle shake

Hold the wig from the cap and give it one gentle shake over a white surface. A small number of loose hairs — typically fewer than 10 — is within the normal range for a new wig as manufacturing-loose hairs work free. Photograph what you find.

Density & Shedding — Quick Reference
🟢 Normal
A few loose hairs on initial shake · Slight compression from shipping — hair will regain volume · Fuller at the crown, slightly less so at nape
🟡 Monitor
10–20 hairs on first shake — document, re-evaluate after the first gentle wash
🔴 Contact Support
Significant shedding in clumps on first handling · Visibly thin or bald patches before any wear

Step 9 — Note Any Scent

Human hair wigs can carry a faint manufacturing or packaging scent, particularly around the wefts. This is common and typically resolves after the first wash.

Scent — Quick Reference
🟢 Normal
Faint, non-specific manufacturing or packaging scent that diminishes when aired
🟡 Monitor
Noticeable but not harsh — allow 30 minutes airing before re-evaluating
🔴 Contact Support
Strong, persistent chemical or mildew odor that does not diminish after airing — document before washing

Step 10 — Try On Without Altering

1

Place the wig on your head without glue or tape

Adjust the internal straps to fit your head. Hold the lace flat against your hairline without securing it permanently. Assess cap fit and comfort.

2

Photograph how it sits

Take front, side, and back photos while wearing the wig unaltered. These create your pre-installation reference.

3

Remove without cutting

If you are satisfied with the inspection, you may proceed to installation. If anything concerns you, remove the wig without cutting and follow the guidance below.

Complete Arrival Checklist

Work through each item before proceeding to installation. Photograph each step.

  • Photographed outer shipping box before opening
  • Photographed sealed inner packaging before opening
  • Confirmed all accessories are present (see full item list)
  • Photographed complete unboxed contents
  • Inspected lace — no tears or holes confirmed
  • Inspected weft stitching — even and secure
  • Inspected adjustable straps and combs — functional
  • Confirmed elastic band intact
  • Evaluated color under natural light — within expected range
  • Measured length — within acceptable variance
  • Checked density visually — consistent and full
  • Performed gentle shake test — shedding within normal range
  • Assessed scent — within normal range or diminishing
  • Tried on without altering — cap fit acceptable
  • Photographed front, side, and back while wearing unaltered

If something concerns you: Do not alter the wig. Keep all original packaging. Photograph the concern clearly under natural light. Then contact the AA WIGS support team at the contact page with your order number and photos. Having an unaltered unit and clear photographs is the strongest position from which support can be provided.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why should I inspect before installing?

Once you cut the lace or wash the wig, determining the original condition of the unit becomes significantly more difficult. This inspection creates a clear record before any permanent changes are made — the strongest position from which to request support if needed.

Is a slight smell normal?

A faint manufacturing or packaging scent is within normal range and typically resolves after the first wash. A strong chemical odor that does not improve after airing should be documented and reported before any alteration is made.

How much shedding is normal at first?

A small number of loose hairs — typically fewer than 10 — on the first gentle handling is within the normal manufacturing range. These are hairs that were not fully secured during construction working free. Shedding that continues in larger amounts after the first wash or that comes from entire sections of the weft is outside normal range.
